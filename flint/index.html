Preventing Water Damage in Flint Homes

A large share of the jobs we attend in Flint began as a minor fault that could have been caught. A few minutes of checking a couple of times a year catches most of it.

Start with supply lines. The rubber hoses on washing machines, dishwashers, toilets and ice makers wear out, and braided stainless replacements are inexpensive. Look under every sink for stains, swollen cabinet floors or a musty smell. At the water heater, watch for corrosion or moisture around the bottom, and flush some sediment out annually. Outside, keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts send water away from the foundation.

Find the main water shutoff now, and show every adult in the home how to close it. Leak sensors placed by the water heater, washer and under sinks cost little and will alert you early. A stain that spreads or a floor that feels soft is worth a call, and Flint homeowners are welcome to ask us about it.

Water Damage We See Most in Flint

Water damage does not always arrive as a sudden gush across the kitchen. Here is what homeowners around Flint phone us about most, along with what usually sits behind each one.

Hidden leaks: Small drips from a worn fitting or a tiny hole in a pipe may go unnoticed behind drywall for a long time. Watch for a water bill that climbs for no reason, soft spots on the floor, and paint that blisters. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. This is Category 3 water, which carries bacteria and should not be handled without protective gear. Porous items it touches, such as carpet and pad, generally have to be removed.

Storms: During a heavy storm, runoff may collect along the foundation faster than drains can carry it off. It enters at door thresholds, low windows and the garage, wherever the ground slopes toward the building. Because surface water carries dirt and chemicals from the ground it passed over, it is handled as unsanitary. Homes across Flint can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or a leaking pipe overhead keeps the air below your floors humid. Over time that leads to sagging insulation, musty odors indoors and soft or cupped flooring. Our team removes the water, dries the framing and can point out what is letting moisture in.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. It is meant to exit through a narrow drain tube, and that tube plugs up with slime and debris. When it backs up, the pan overflows into the ceiling, closet or hallway around the air handler. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.
