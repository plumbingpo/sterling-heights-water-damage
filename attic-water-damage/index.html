Attic Water Damage: What Sterling Heights Homeowners Should Know

Water reaches an attic from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. Our team in Sterling Heights inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • Dripping, rusted or water marked ducts and metal hardware up in the attic
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that makes it through the roof falls into the insulation and is absorbed. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold May Begin

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. The sheathing, along with the top face of the drywall ceiling, is where growth can begin.

A few weeks on

Ceiling Stains Appear

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Rot Sets In

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Air Conditioner Drips

Air handlers, with their drain pans and condensate lines, sit in many attics. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

A bathroom fan that vents into the attic instead of outdoors adds warm, moist air every day. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.

Ceiling Collapse

Saturated insulation is heavy. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Mold Under the Roof Deck

The underside of the roof deck is a common place for mold when ventilation is poor. Before anyone looks, it may already have spread over a large section.
