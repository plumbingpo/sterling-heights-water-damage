Appliance Leak Water Damage in Sterling Heights: What to Know

Most of the appliance leaks we see trace back to three machines. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Cleanup is different for each of them.

For homeowners in Sterling Heights, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Then our team removes the standing water, meters the materials that soaked it up, and places equipment matched to the size of the damage.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. It may be requested by the manufacturer of the appliance and by your insurance company.
Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Aging rubber supply hoses on a washing machine, which weaken and then split under pressure.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
  • Worn dishwasher door gaskets, cracked drain hoses or a failed pump seal.
  • Ice maker lines, copper or plastic, that crack, kink or come loose where they connect.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Kitchen Cabinets

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. Panels swell and come apart, and a wet cabinet box can keep the wall behind it damp for weeks.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Rooms Next Door

A washer hose can release water for hours. It passes under walls into hallways, closets and bedrooms, and soaks carpet and pad well away from the laundry room.

Wall Bases and Trim

On a flooded kitchen or laundry floor, the baseboards and the lower edge of the drywall draw water upward. The wall behind the appliance is often the wettest because nobody could see it.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Pull and Inspect

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

2

Remove Standing Water

Carpet and floors are cleared of standing water with extraction equipment. To reach water caught below cabinets or under floating floors, our crew pulls toe kicks or takes up boards.

3

Dry in Place

Dehumidifiers and air movers are set up, and when it is possible, directed airflow dries hardwood and cabinets where they sit so nothing has to be torn out.

4

Clean and Confirm

Surfaces touched by washer or dishwasher discharge get antimicrobial treatment. We take final moisture readings before the appliance goes back so it is not set on a damp floor.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental events, and a burst hose or a machine overflowing with no warning is that kind of event. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

Pricing

How the Quote Is Built

  • Which appliance leaked and whether it was a fast flood or a slow seep.
  • Removal of cabinets or flooring, compared with drying them in place when that is possible.
  • The number of rooms affected, counting a ceiling under a laundry on an upper floor.
  • How long the leak ran before it was found, especially with a slow drip behind an appliance.
