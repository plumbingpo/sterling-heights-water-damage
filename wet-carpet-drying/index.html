Wet Carpet Drying Help in Sterling Heights

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. In Sterling Heights, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

What It Looks Like

  • A clammy, cool patch underfoot that lingers well past the original spill
  • A carpet corner where the backing comes apart from the face when pulled up
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • A sour, musty smell that gets stronger when the room is closed up
  • Yellow or brown discoloration rising to the fiber tips while the carpet dries
Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Furniture Marks

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Damp Base of the Walls

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Mold on the Backing

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Water that started clean does not stay that way. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Past Saving

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

What We Do

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.
