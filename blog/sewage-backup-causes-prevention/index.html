Why Sewage Backups Happen in Sterling Heights, MI

Home / Blog / Sewage Backup Causes and Prevention in Sterling Heights, MI

Few household emergencies are as unpleasant as sewage backing up into the home. Raw sewage entering your living spaces creates immediate health hazards, causes extensive property damage, and requires professional cleanup that can cost thousands of dollars. Understanding why sewage backups occur and taking preventive measures can significantly reduce your risk. Below, Sterling Heights Restoration explains what usually causes them for Sterling Heights, MI homeowners, plus the practical things you can do to protect your home.

Why Sewage Backups Happen

A backup happens when wastewater leaving your property cannot reach the septic tank or the municipal sewer system the way it should. Usual culprits are tree roots growing into sewer pipes; lines clogged by a buildup of hair, grease, and debris; pipes that are old or have collapsed; combined sewer overflow events; an overloaded municipal system in heavy rain; and toilets used to flush the wrong things, such as feminine products, wipes, and too much paper.

Tree Root Intrusion

In Sterling Heights, many blocked sewer lines turn out to be the work of tree roots. A sewer pipe is a steady supply of moisture, which is exactly what roots go looking for. They work their way in at cracks, joints, and connection points, then slowly fill the inside of the pipe until it blocks and backs up. Older sewer lines made of clay tile are especially easy for roots to invade. Schedule a sewer camera inspection every 2 to 3 years so root trouble is found ahead of any backup.

Ways to Lower Your Backup Risk

To guard a Sterling Heights house against backups, start by having a backwater valve fitted to your sewer connection so the city system cannot flow back in. Grease and cooking oil should never go down a drain. Do not flush feminine products, wipes, or anything other than toilet paper. Have the sewer line inspected and cleaned on a regular schedule. Deal with a slow drain quickly, before it turns into a full blockage. In an emergency someone will need the sewer cleanout, so learn where yours sits. Consider sewer line insurance or a sewage backup endorsement on your homeowner's policy.

What to Do If Sewage Backs Up

Cleaning up a sewage backup on your own is something you should never try. Sewage contains dangerous pathogens including bacteria, viruses, and parasites that can cause serious illness. Get everyone out of the affected area right away, do not touch contaminated materials or water, and phone Sterling Heights Restoration at (352) 610-8099 so a professional crew can start emergency cleanup.
