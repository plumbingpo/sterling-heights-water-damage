Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. When it happens in Sterling Heights, Sterling Heights Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home

Sewage Cleanup FAQ

Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Grease buildup, wipes and other items that should not be flushed, and tree roots growing into the pipe are the usual causes. Other possibilities are a failed old pipe and a municipal sewer overloaded by heavy rain, which sends sewage up through floor drains. To know for certain, have the line camera-inspected once cleanup is finished.
Temporary relocation is what we usually recommend, particularly for a backup that contaminated a large part of the home. Both the decontamination chemicals and pathogens in the air can be a risk to your health. After seeing your situation firsthand, our team tells you what makes sense.
Emergency sewage removal and decontamination typically takes 1 to 3 days to complete. How extensive the damage is determines the rest, and restoring everything with new materials may need 1 to 3 weeks. Our first priority is making the property safe to occupy again as fast as we can.

What each item is made of decides that. Glass, sealed tile, metal and other hard surfaces that do not absorb water are normally salvageable after disinfection. The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
