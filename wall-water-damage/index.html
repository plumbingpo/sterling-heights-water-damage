Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Sterling Heights

Think of drywall as a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

Warning Signs

  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Trim along the floor that has puffed up, twisted or come loose
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Mildew smell you notice most when standing close to one section of wall
  • Discolored lower drywall with a visible line marking how high water wicked
How We Fix It

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Cut or Ventilate

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Walls You Cannot See

Sections of wall covered by cabinets, a vanity or shelving stay damp because air never reaches them. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Damage Reaches the Studs

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.
